   - Prizes for professional entertainers in competitions: 9 FAM 402.2-5(G)(2) and CBP Q6.

# U.S. visitor (B-1, B-2, VWP/ESTA) status rules: a knowledge base with citations

This is general information, not legal advice. It reflects these sources as of 2026-10-02:
- 9 FAM 402.2 (CT:VISA-2193, 03-19-2026);
- the CBP "B-1 Permissible Activities" sheet (updated Jan 2026);
- eCFR Title 8 (current to 2026-09-30).

All three were re-checked on 2026-10-02.

**Labels used below.**
- **B1** means a B-1 visa holder admitted for business. **B2** means a B-2 visa holder admitted as a tourist.
- **VWP** means a Visa Waiver Program / ESTA traveler. CBP admits these travelers as **WB** (business) or **WT** (tourism).
- **Severity:**
  - *critical* means it can lead to removal, re-entry bars, or loss of the visa or VWP.
  - *high* means a likely status violation.
  - *medium* means a gray area or a risk in the evidence.
  - *low* means informational.

## Rules

### A. Work and money

**1. `no-us-paid-work`: Paid work for a US company or person**
- **Applies to:** B1, B2, VWP. **Severity:** critical.
- **Rule:**
  - B-2 visitors "may not engage in any employment."
  - Other nonimmigrants, including B-1, may work only if their status authorizes it.
  - A business visitor may not receive a salary from a US source. US-source money is limited to these narrow cases:
    - reimbursement of actual incidental expenses;
    - INA 212(q) academic honoraria (rule 13);
    - prize money in the athlete and entertainer competition cases (rule 15);
    - an outside director's pay for time and travel to attend a US corporation's board meetings ([CBP-B1] Q20).
- **Violations:** a paid consulting day for a US startup; going onto a US payroll; a paid shift or "paid trial week"; filling out a W-4 or I-9.
- **Instead:** decline. Do the work only after leaving the US, under a foreign arrangement. Get a work visa (H, O, L, etc.) before starting.
- **Cite:** [CFR214.1] §214.1(e)(1)-(2); INA 101(a)(15)(B); [9FAM402.2] 402.2-5(A), 402.2-5(F)(1); [CBP-B1] Q1, Q20.

**2. `no-freelance-contract-in-us`: Freelance, contract or gig work done while in the US**
- **Applies to:** B1, B2, VWP. **Severity:** critical.
- **Rule:**
  - Calling yourself a contractor changes nothing. B status excludes "skilled or unskilled labor" in the US.
  - Business activity is acceptable only when it is incidental to work done mainly abroad, with the principal place of business and the profits abroad (the *Matter of Hira* test).
  - The 8 CFR 274a.1 definitions (employer sanctions) leave independent contractors out of "employee." But contractor status is decided case by case "regardless of what the individual or entity calls itself" (274a.1(j)). The B-status bar on labor applies either way.
- **Violations:** Upwork or Fiverr jobs for US clients done from a US rental; a design contract with a US agency; rideshare or delivery driving; teaching paid classes.
- **Instead:** meet the client and negotiate the contract while visiting, then deliver the work from abroad.
- **Cite:** INA 101(a)(15)(B); [9FAM402.2] 402.2-5(A)(a)-(b), 402.2-5(C)(a); [CFR274a.1] (h), (j); [CBP-B1] Q1.

**3. `one-off-paid-gigs-and-user-studies`: Small one-off paid tasks, bounties and paid user studies**
- **Applies to:** B1, B2, VWP. **Severity:** high.
- **Rule:**
  - No source creates a small-amount exception, so one paid task done in the US for a US payer is unauthorized work.
  - Gift cards and other in-kind payments should be treated as pay. This is an inference from "wages or other remuneration"; no source addresses it directly.
- **Uncertain:** paid research or usability sessions. No official source addresses them, so treat them as risky.
- **Violations:** $200 to test a prototype at a company's US office; a paid photo shoot; a paid talk at a for-profit event; a paid micro-task or bug bounty while in the US.
- **Instead:** take part unpaid, or with receipt-based expenses only. Or do the paid session remotely after you leave.
- **Cite:** [CFR214.1] (e)(1); [9FAM402.2] 402.2-5(F)(1); [CFR274a.1] (f)-(g).

**4. `no-unpaid-productive-work`: Unpaid work that would normally be paid**
- **Applies to:** B1, B2, VWP. **Severity:** high.
- **Rule:** Working for free does not make labor allowed.
  - The State Department says work classifiable as H-2 stays H-2 even when the person "is working without compensation."
  - A performer who is normally paid cannot use B-2 by agreeing to perform for free.
  - Watching how a business operates is allowed; producing work is not.
- **Violations:** an unpaid internship; building product features for a friend's startup; work-exchange (labor for room and board, which is in-kind pay); unpaid trial shifts.
- **Instead:** observe, attend meetings, give feedback in meetings. Training or internships need J-1 or H-3.
- **Cite:** [9FAM402.2] 402.2-5(F) paragraph a(3), 402.2-4(A)(7); [CBP-B1] Q12, Q17.

**5. `remote-work-foreign-employer`: Working remotely for your foreign employer while visiting (gray area)**
- **Applies to:** B1, B2, VWP. **Severity:** medium (uncertain).
- **Rule:** None of the sources checked (INA, 8 CFR, 9 FAM 402.2, the CBP B-1 sheet) directly allows or forbids remote work for a foreign employer. Two principles pull in different directions:
  - Brief tasks that are incidental to a job based abroad fit the *Hira* logic.
  - Sustained full-time work from US soil can look like labor "within the United States" and like living here. CBP says (about athletes) it is "not permissible to, in effect, live and work in the United States."
  - Officers decide case by case. Answer CBP truthfully.
- **Violations:** working full-time for your home-country employer during a 90-day VWP stay ("digital nomad"); taking on US clients while visiting.
- **Instead:** treat the trip as vacation or B-1 activity. Limit work to occasional urgent email. For long remote stays, get advice from an immigration attorney.
- **Cite:** INA 101(a)(15)(B); [CFR274a.1] (h); [9FAM402.2] 402.2-5(A)(b); [CBP-B1] Q3.

**6. `us-bank-account-payments`: Receiving payments into US accounts or apps**
- **Applies to:** B1, B2, VWP. **Severity:** medium.
- **Rule:**
  - Having a US account is not itself prohibited.
  - Incoming payments from US payers for services (Zelle, Venmo, PayPal, Stripe, a 1099 or W-2) are direct evidence of US-source pay. Visitors may not receive that beyond actual expenses, except in the narrow cases in rule 1.
  - Visitors generally cannot get a work-authorized Social Security number. The narrow exceptions are in 402.2-5(I).
- **Violations:** invoicing a US client and being paid during the visit; receiving a 1099.
- **Instead:** take only receipt-based reimbursements, and keep your salary with your foreign employer.
- **Cite:** [9FAM402.2] 402.2-5(F)(1), 402.2-5(I).

**7. `no-us-business-operations`: Running or starting a US business**
- **Applies to:** B1, B2, VWP. **Severity:** high.
- **Rule:**
  - A business visitor may look for investments, meet partners, negotiate and attend board meetings.
  - A visitor may not do productive labor in, or actively manage, a US business.
  - A visitor may not be admitted to start or work in a new US branch.
- **Violations:** running your US LLC day to day; staffing your store; working in your newly opened US office.
- **Instead:** E-2 status (CBP Q8) or L-1 status (CBP Q11).
- **Cite:** [CBP-B1] Q8, Q11; [9FAM402.2] 402.2-5(B), 402.2-5(C)(7).

### B. Permitted activities and their limits

**8. `b1-permitted-business-activities`: What business visitors may do**
- **Applies to:** B1, VWP (when admitted as WB). **Severity:** low.
- **Rule:** With no US-source salary, a business visitor may:
  - consult with business associates and negotiate contracts;
  - attend scientific, educational, professional or business conventions, conferences and seminars;
  - litigate, or do independent research;
  - settle an estate (listed on the USCIS B-1 page);
  - attend meetings of a US company's board, as a board member. CBP Q20 says an outside director may also be paid by the corporation for time and travel to attend board meetings;
  - take orders for goods made abroad.
- **Where it goes wrong:** a "meeting" turns into doing the work, such as coding at the client's office.
- **Instead:** carry an agenda, invitation letters and a letter from your foreign employer.
- **Cite:** [9FAM402.2] 402.2-5(B), 402.2-5(C)(3); [CBP-B1] Q1, Q20; [USCIS-B1].

**9. `tourist-admission-no-business`: A tourist admission is not a business trip**
- **Applies to:** B2, VWP. **Severity:** medium.
- **Rule:**
  - CBP says: "You may not engage in B-1 business activities while admitted under a B-2 visa."
  - VWP travelers are admitted as WB (business) or WT (tourist). In practice the class follows the purpose stated at entry.
- **Uncertain:** casual networking during a mainly tourist trip.
- **Violations:** entering "on vacation" and then spending the week in client negotiations or at a trade show.
- **Instead:** hold a B-1/B-2 visa, or tell CBP your business purpose at entry.
- **Cite:** [CBP-B1] Q18; [CFR214.1] (a)(2) (WB/WT designations).

**10. `job-interviews-and-networking`: Job interviews, meetups and networking**
- **Applies to:** B1, VWP (WB); B2 is less clear. **Severity:** low.
- **Rule:**
  - No regulation or FAM section specifically mentions job interviews. They are widely treated as allowed because no work is done.
  - The closest official parallel: amateur athletes may attend brief professional try-outs on B-1, with only incidental expenses paid. B-1 no longer fits once the athlete is hired.
  - You may not start work, onboarding or a paid "trial project," or stay to wait for a work visa.
  - VWP travelers cannot change status inside the US.
- **Violations:** interviewing and then starting a take-home paid trial in the US; accepting a job and staying on.
- **Instead:** do interviews and meetups only, with expenses reimbursed. Leave the US and get the work visa from abroad.
- **Cite:** [9FAM402.2] 402.2-5(B), 402.2-5(C)(4) paragraph c; [CBP-B1] Q5; [INA248] (a)(4).

**11. `volunteering-limits`: Volunteering**
- **Applies to:** B1, B2, VWP. **Severity:** medium.
- **Rule:**
  - Allowed: some volunteer services, such as attending meetings, speaking at a conference, or helping at a religious event.
  - Not allowed: coming to live in the US or to provide labor for a charity, and construction volunteering.
  - Separately, B-1 visitors may not perform building or construction work at all. They may only supervise or train others.
  - A formal voluntary-service program requires all of these:
    - B-1 status;
    - membership in, and commitment to, a recognized religious or nonprofit charitable organization;
    - a program that helps the needy or a religious or charitable cause, and does not involve selling articles or soliciting donations;
    - no US pay beyond expenses;
    - a written statement from the sponsor.
- **Violations:** months of full-time NGO work; volunteer house-building; "volunteering" at a for-profit event in exchange for perks.
- **Instead:** keep to occasional charity events, or use a formal B-1 voluntary-service program.
- **Cite:** [CBP-B1] Q17; [9FAM402.2] 402.2-5(C)(2); [CFR214.2b] (b)(5) (construction only).

**12. `study-recreational-only`: Studying**
- **Applies to:** B1, B2, VWP. **Severity:** high.
- **Rule:**
  - Enrolling in a course of study violates B status.
  - A short recreational course that earns no credit toward a degree is allowed, for example the State Department's "two-day cooking class while on vacation."
  - The FAM also allows B-2 classification when avocational or recreational study is the main purpose of the trip.
  - Credit-bearing or degree study needs F-1 or M-1, approved before you start.
- **Uncertain:** full-time vocational bootcamps may need M-1.
- **Violations:** a semester-long English program; a credit-bearing summer course.
- **Instead:** take weekend workshops or a two-day class. Get an F-1 or M-1 visa from abroad. B visa holders (not VWP) can instead file an I-539 change of status, and must not enroll until it is approved.
- **Cite:** [CFR214.2b] (b)(7); [9FAM402.2] 402.2-4(A)(6), 402.2-4(B)(9); [DOS-Visitor]; [USCIS-COS].

**13. `honoraria-212q`: Academic honoraria**
- **Applies to:** B1, VWP (WB). B2 and WT are uncertain (see caveat 2). **Severity:** medium.
- **Rule:** A visitor may accept an honorarium plus incidental expenses for usual academic activity (lecturing, guest teaching, an academic festival) only if all of these hold:
  - the activity lasts no more than 9 days at any single institution;
  - the payer is a higher-education institution or a related or affiliated nonprofit, a nonprofit research organization, or a government research organization (INA 212(p)(1));
  - the work benefits that institution;
  - you have not taken such payments from more than 5 institutions in the previous 6 months.
- **Violations:** an honorarium from a for-profit company for a talk; a sixth honorarium within 6 months; a 2-week paid lecture series.
- **Instead:** from for-profit hosts, take expenses only. Keep a count of honoraria.
- **Cite:** [INA212] (q) and (p)(1); [9FAM402.2] 402.2-5(F)(2).

**14. `expense-reimbursement-only`: Reimbursement of incidental expenses**
- **Applies to:** B1, B2, VWP. **Severity:** low.
- **Rule:**
  - A US host may pay or reimburse actual, reasonable costs of travel to and from the event, plus living costs: meals, lodging, laundry and other basic services.
  - The incidental expenses "may not exceed the actual reasonable expenses."
  - Flat "stipends" or per diems well above real costs look like pay.
- **Violations:** a $1,000 "speaker stipend" from a startup.
- **Instead:** receipt-based reimbursement, or have the host book travel directly.
- **Cite:** [9FAM402.2] 402.2-5(F)(1), 402.2-4(A)(7).

**15. `contests-and-prizes`: Competitions, hackathons and prize money (uncertain)**
- **Applies to:** B1, B2, VWP. **Severity:** medium.
- **Rule:**
  - On B-2, amateurs may compete in contests without pay, and expenses may be reimbursed.
  - Professional entertainers may be classified B-1 for a competition with no pay other than "a prize (monetary or otherwise)" and expenses. CBP Q6 says a prize is acceptable "in certain limited instances."
  - Professional athletes may receive "prize money" for tournaments and sporting events, on these conditions:
    - their principal place of business is abroad;
    - their salary accrues mainly abroad;
    - per CBP, they are not, in effect, living and working in the US, including where prize money is the main source of income.
  - No official source addresses hackathons. Whether a prize counts as "payment" for a B-2 or VWP amateur is unresolved.
  - Prizes from a US-resident grantor are US-source income and generally subject to 30% chapter 3 withholding.
- **Violations:** relying on prize money as income while staying in the US.
- **Cite:** [9FAM402.2] 402.2-4(A)(7), 402.2-5(C)(4) paragraph a, 402.2-5(G)(2); [CBP-B1] Q3, Q6; [DOS-Visitor]; [IRS-515].

**16. `sponsored-challenges-disguised-work`: Hackathons or challenges that are really commissioned work**
- **Applies to:** B1, B2, VWP. **Severity:** high (an inference; no direct guidance).
- **Rule:** The prize looks like payment for services done in the US if the "competition" means any of these:
  - building deliverables for the sponsor;
  - handing over your IP;
  - continuing work after the event.
- **Violations:** "Build our integration; the winner gets $5k and we keep the code"; a paid pilot after the event; bounty programs during the stay.
- **Instead:** read the rules on IP and decline IP assignment. Do any follow-up paid work only from abroad or with a proper visa.
- **Cite:** [9FAM402.2] 402.2-5(A); [CFR214.1] (e).

### C. Length of stay

**17. `vwp-90-day-max`: VWP is capped at 90 days**
- **Applies to:** VWP. **Severity:** critical.
- **Rule:** VWP admission is for no more than 90 days. The admit-until date on your I-94 controls.
- **Violations:** staying 91 or more days.
- **Instead:** check your I-94 and plan to leave before the date.
- **Cite:** [INA217] (a)(1); [CBP-VWP]; [I94].

**18. `vwp-no-extension-no-change-of-status`: VWP cannot be extended or converted**
- **Applies to:** VWP. **Severity:** critical.
- **Rule:**
  - No extension of stay is available.
  - Only in an emergency may DHS grant up to 30 days of "satisfactory departure."
  - You cannot change to another nonimmigrant status, except T or U.
  - You cannot adjust to permanent residence unless you are an immediate relative of a US citizen or have an approved VAWA self-petition.
- **Violations:** filing an I-539 to extend; switching to F-1 or H-1B inside the US.
- **Instead:** leave and apply for the right visa abroad.
- **Cite:** [CFR214.1] (c)(3)(i); [CFR217] §217.3(a); [INA248] (a)(4); [INA245c]; [USCIS-Extend]; [USCIS-COS].

**19. `vwp-adjacent-trips-no-reset`: Trips to Canada, Mexico or nearby islands do not reset the VWP clock**
- **Applies to:** VWP. **Severity:** high.
- **Rule:** After a trip to Canada, Mexico or an adjacent island, you may be readmitted only "for the balance" of your original VWP admission period.
- **Violations:** going to Cancún on day 80 and expecting a fresh 90 days.
- **Instead:**
  - Keep the whole stay within 90 days.
  - Any new admission after a true departure abroad is up to CBP.
  - Residents of those countries may be treated differently: the return-ticket rule in §217.2(c)(1) has a resident exception. Confirm with CBP.
- **Cite:** [CFR217] §217.3(b), §217.2(c)(1).

**20. `b-i94-admit-until`: For B visas, the I-94 date controls, not the visa's expiry**
- **Applies to:** B1, B2. **Severity:** critical.
- **Rule:**
  - Your authorized stay ends on the I-94 admit-until date.
  - Under the regulation, B-2 visitors are admitted for at least 6 months (exceptions need specific approval).
  - Any B visitor may be admitted for no more than 1 year.
  - USCIS's B-1 page says B-1 initial admission is generally "1 to 6 months; 6 months is the maximum," which reflects practice. The regulation's ceiling is 1 year.
  - A visa's validity says nothing about how long you may stay.
- **Violations:** staying until the visa expires; assuming 6 months without checking.
- **Instead:** check [I94] after every entry and get errors corrected.
- **Cite:** [CFR214.2b] (b)(1)-(2); [USCIS-Extend]; [USCIS-B1]; [9FAM402.2] 402.2-4(B)(5); [9FAM302.1] 302.1-9(B)(1) paragraph c(2).

**21. `b-extension-i539`: Extending a B stay**
- **Applies to:** B1, B2. **Severity:** high.
- **Rule:**
  - File Form I-539 before the I-94 date; USCIS suggests at least 45 days before.
  - Each extension is 6 months at most.
  - You are not eligible if you violated the conditions of your admission, for example by working.
  - **While a timely, nonfrivolous I-539 is pending and you have not worked without authorization:**
    - DHS treats the whole time the application is pending as authorized stay, so no unlawful presence builds up toward the 3-year or 10-year bar.
    - The statute separately guarantees up to 120 days of tolling, for the 3-year bar only.
- **Violations:** filing after the I-94 date; working while the application is pending.
- **Instead:** file early, and don't work.
- **Cite:** [CFR214.2b] (b)(1); [CFR214.1] (c)(4); [USCIS-I539]; [USCIS-Extend]; [INA212] (a)(9)(B)(iv); [9FAM302.11] 302.11-3(B)(5).

**22. `no-new-activity-before-cos-approval`: Changing your purpose mid-visit**
- **Applies to:** B1, B2. **Severity:** high.
- **Rule:**
  - If your purpose changes, for example to study, you must file for a change of status before your stay expires.
  - You must not start the new activity until USCIS approves it.
  - A B-1 visitor may switch to tourism without filing, before the authorized stay expires.
  - VWP travelers cannot change status at all.
- **Violations:** starting classes while the change-of-status application is pending.
- **Instead:** wait for approval, or leave and apply for the new visa abroad.
- **Cite:** [USCIS-COS]; [CFR214.2b] (b)(7).

### D. Consequences of overstaying or violating status

**23. `overstay-unlawful-presence-bars`: Unlawful presence and re-entry bars**
- **Applies to:** B1, B2, VWP. **Severity:** critical.
- **Rule:**
  - Unlawful presence generally starts the day after the I-94 date. Time before age 18 does not count.
  - More than 180 days and less than 1 year in a single stay, then leaving voluntarily before removal proceedings start, means a 3-year bar.
  - 1 year or more in a single stay means a 10-year bar.
  - More than 1 year in total (aggregate), or a prior removal order, followed by entering or trying to enter without being admitted, means a permanent bar. It can be lifted only with DHS consent after 10 years outside the US.
- **Cite:** [INA212] (a)(9)(B)-(C); [USCIS-UP].

**24. `overstay-visa-void-222g`: Overstaying voids your visa**
- **Applies to:** B1, B2. **Severity:** critical.
- **Rule:**
  - Staying past the authorized stay, even by one day, automatically voids the visa used to enter, including a multiple-entry visa.
  - A formal status-violation finding by USCIS, an immigration judge or the BIA has the same effect.
  - You then generally have to reapply in your country of nationality.
- **Cite:** [INA222g]; [9FAM302.1] 302.1-9; [DOS-Visitor].

**25. `vwp-violation-ineligibility`: Overstaying or violating VWP ends future VWP use**
- **Applies to:** VWP. **Severity:** critical.
- **Rule:** Anyone who did not comply with the conditions of a previous VWP admission (an overstay, or unauthorized work) cannot use the VWP again and must get a visa.
- **Cite:** [INA217] (a)(7).

**26. `vwp-waiver-of-rights`: VWP travelers give up appeal and hearing rights**
- **Applies to:** VWP. **Severity:** high.
- **Rule:** VWP travelers waive the right to appeal an admissibility decision and to contest removal, except through asylum. CBP or DHS can refuse entry or remove you without a judge.
- **Cite:** [INA217] (b); [CFR217] §217.4.

### E. Honesty, intent and evidence

**27. `truthful-statements-misrepresentation`: Tell the truth, consistently**
- **Applies to:** B1, B2, VWP. **Severity:** critical.
- **Rule:**
  - Willfully misrepresenting a material fact to get a visa or admission makes you permanently inadmissible, with only limited waivers.
  - Willfully failing to give DHS full and truthful information is a status violation even if the fact is not material.
  - Keep the visa application, ESTA, answers to CBP and your actual activities consistent.
- **Violations:** saying "tourism" while carrying a client contract; giving the visa officer and CBP different stories.
- **Instead:** state your real purpose and carry documents that support it.
- **Cite:** [INA212] (a)(6)(C)(i); [CFR214.1] (f).

**28. `ninety-day-rule`: Conduct within 90 days of entry is presumed to show your intent**
- **Applies to:** B1, B2, VWP (when later applying for a visa). **Severity:** high.
- **Rule:**
  - Within 90 days of the visa application or of entry, any of these lets a consular officer presume you lied:
    - working without authorization;
    - enrolling in study;
    - marrying a US citizen or permanent resident and taking up residence;
    - doing anything else that needs a change or adjustment of status.
  - You have the burden of rebutting that presumption.
- **Cite:** [9FAM302.9] 302.9-4(B)(3) paragraphs g-h.

**29. `immigrant-intent-residence-abroad`: Keep your home abroad and a clear end date**
- **Applies to:** B1, B2, VWP. **Severity:** high.
- **Rule:**
  - You must keep a residence abroad that you do not intend to abandon.
  - You must plan a stay of specific, limited length.
  - If you fail either requirement, a visa is refused under INA 214(b).
- **Violations:** selling your home abroad; signing a long-term US lease; getting a US driver's license; shipping your belongings.
- **Cite:** [9FAM402.2] 402.2-2(B)-(D).

**30. `frequent-or-long-visits`: Repeated long visits can look like living in the US**
- **Applies to:** B1, B2, VWP. **Severity:** medium.
- **Rule:** Back-to-back long stays can lead CBP to conclude you live in the US. No numeric rule was found in the statute, regulations or guidance checked; "6 months in any 12" is folklore.
- **Violations:** 6 months in, a 1-week trip out, then back for another 6 months, repeatedly.
- **Instead:** show strong ties abroad, and make your time abroad much longer than your time in the US.
- **Cite:** [9FAM402.2] 402.2-2(B)-(D); [CBP-B1] Q3.

**31. `marriage-while-visiting`: Engagement and marriage during a visit**
- **Applies to:** B1, B2, VWP. **Severity:** high.
- **Rule:**
  - On B-2 you may meet your fiancé(e)'s family, get engaged, plan a wedding, or marry, if you intend to return to a residence abroad soon after the marriage.
  - Coming to marry a US citizen and stay requires a K-1 or an immigrant visa.
  - Marrying and taking up residence within 90 days triggers the misrepresentation presumption.
  - VWP travelers can adjust status only as immediate relatives of a US citizen (or as approved VAWA self-petitioners). Intent at entry is examined.
- **Cite:** [9FAM402.2] 402.2-4(B)(1)-(2); [9FAM302.9] 302.9-4(B)(3) paragraph g(2)(b)(iii); [INA245c].

**32. `birth-tourism`: Travelling to give birth in the US**
- **Applies to:** B2; VWP is governed by DHS standards. **Severity:** medium.
- **Rule:**
  - Travel mainly to give birth in the US so the child gets citizenship is not a valid B-2 purpose.
  - Consular officers presume that purpose when they have reason to believe the applicant will give birth during the stay.
  - The presumption can be rebutted, for example by a need for specialized medical care that is not available near home.
- **Cite:** [9FAM402.2] 402.2-4(A)(8).

**33. `social-media-and-devices`: Social media posts and devices can be checked**
- **Applies to:** B1, B2, VWP. **Severity:** medium.
- **Rule:**
  - CBP may search phones and laptops at the border.
  - ESTA asks for social media accounts; the question is currently optional.
  - In Dec 2025, CBP published a Paperwork Reduction Act information-collection Notice (90 FR 57208), not a rulemaking. It said social media from the last 5 years would become a mandatory ESTA data element. Comments closed Feb 9, 2026.
  - As of 2026-10-02, OMB's ICR history for control no. 1651-0111 shows no completed approval after March 2025, so implementation is unconfirmed.
  - Posts like "started freelancing from NYC," "joined X as a contractor" or "open to US gigs" are evidence of work or of misrepresentation.
- **Instead:** post only what is true and consistent with the purpose of your visit. Do not announce work you are not authorized to do.
- **Cite:** [CBP-Devices]; [FR-ESTA]; [OMB-ESTA].

## Answers to the two questions

**(a) Can a B-2 or VWP visitor accept a small paid task from a US company after attending its event?**

Generally no. B-2 visitors "may not engage in any employment," and no source exempts small or one-off tasks. A business visitor (B-1 or WB) also may not take a US-source salary or pay for services. Payments into a US account add evidence.

US-source money is allowed only in narrow cases, and none of them covers a for-profit company's task:
- reimbursement of actual travel and living costs;
- an INA 212(q) academic honorarium (the payer must be an academic, nonprofit-research or government-research body, not a company);
- prize money in the athlete and entertainer competition cases;
- director pay for attending a US corporation's board meetings (CBP Q20).

**Consequences.** Doing the task would be a status violation. It can lead to:
- the 90-day misrepresentation presumption at a later visa interview;
- 222(g) voiding of the visa after a formal finding;
- for VWP travelers, removal without a hearing and permanent loss of VWP eligibility under INA 217(a)(7).

**Safer:** decline, or agree to do the task after leaving, performed and invoiced from abroad. Work done abroad is not "employment" within the United States under [CFR274a.1](h); taxes are a separate question. Accept expense reimbursement only.

**Cite:** [CFR214.1](e)(1); [9FAM402.2] 402.2-5(A), 402.2-5(F)(1)-(2); [INA212](q), (p)(1); [CBP-B1] Q20; [9FAM302.9]; [INA217](a)(7), (b); [INA222g].

**(b) Can they compete in a US hackathon and accept a prize (cash or equipment)?**

- **Competing:** generally yes.
  - Amateurs may enter contests on B-2 without pay, with expenses reimbursed.
  - Attending a hackathon as a professional or conference-style event fits B-1 or WB.
  - If business is part of the purpose, say so to CBP. CBP says B-2 visitors may not do B-1 activities.
- **Taking a prize:** no official source addresses hackathons, so this is uncertain.
  - *Supporting it:*
    - The State Department and CBP allow professional entertainers in competitions a prize "(monetary or otherwise)." CBP says this applies "in certain limited instances."
    - Professional athletes based abroad may receive "prize money" for tournaments. CBP adds that this is not allowed where prize money is the main source of income.
  - *Against it:* the B-2 amateur guidance and the State Department visitor page say amateurs may compete "if not being paid for participating." Whether a merit prize counts as payment is unresolved.
  - Equipment prizes are analyzed the same way as cash. The only explicit "or otherwise" language is in the entertainer provisions.
- **Lower risk:**
  - a one-time, open, merit-judged competition;
  - no IP assignment;
  - no deliverables for the sponsor;
  - no follow-on engagement;
  - prizes are not a regular source of income.
- **Higher risk:** sponsor-commissioned builds, "bounties," IP transfer, paid pilots, or being "hired" out of the event (see rule 16).
- **Tax:**
  - A prize from a US-resident payer is US-source income, generally with 30% chapter 3 withholding unless a tax treaty applies.
  - Expect a W-8BEN form and a 1042-S.
- **Practical:**
  - Keep the official rules and the prize terms.
  - Ask the organizer to confirm the prize is not payment for services.
  - For large or repeated prizes, consult an immigration attorney.

**Cite:** [9FAM402.2] 402.2-4(A)(7), 402.2-5(C)(4) paragraph a, 402.2-5(G)(2); [CBP-B1] Q3, Q6, Q18; [DOS-Visitor]; [IRS-515].

## Open questions and caveats
1. No primary source checked addresses remote work for a foreign employer, hackathon prizes, paid user or research studies, or job interviews. The guidance above reasons from close analogies, and CBP officers have wide discretion.
2. **Honoraria:** the statute covers any alien "admitted under section 1101(a)(15)(B)," which on its face includes B-2 and VWP visitors. The FAM describes it as a B-1 benefit. Whether B-2 or WT visitors can rely on it in practice is unclear.
3. **ESTA social media:** the mandatory 5-year disclosure was announced in a Paperwork Reduction Act Notice (90 FR 57208), not a proposed rule. As of 2026-10-02, OMB's ICR history for 1651-0111 lists no approval after March 2025. Implementation is unconfirmed.
4. **travel.state.gov:** bot protection (HTTP 403) blocked fetching the visitor page. Its wording was confirmed through search results only.
5. Treatment of residents of Canada, Mexico or adjacent islands after a short trip is not settled in the regulation.
6. **B-1 admission length:** USCIS's web page (6 months maximum) and 8 CFR 214.2(b)(1) (up to 1 year) differ. CBP's admission stamp or I-94 controls in each case.
7. The FAM, CBP guidance and USCIS pages change often. Re-check them before each release of the app.
